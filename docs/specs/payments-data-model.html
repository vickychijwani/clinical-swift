Reporting boundary

Cash flow is the useful first view.

A charge creates an amount owed; receiving payment creates incoming cash. Count the receipt once. Equipment purchases reduce cash immediately. Net cash flow is not accounting profit.

Small by design

Chosen for one clinic and a doctor with limited admin time

01 / ONE ENTRY TYPE

Keep the event, derive its effects.

GainOne structure covers fees, receipts, expenses, and corrections. Separate entry kinds keep cash and debt calculations clear.
AcceptSome fields apply only to certain kinds. Validation enforces those rules.
02 / AUTOMATIC ATTRIBUTION

Oldest first, without allocation records.

GainThe doctor enters a receipt once. The app provides sensible coverage across appointments automatically.
AcceptNo manual split or permanent allocation history. Editing ledger entries can change the displayed coverage.
03 / RETAIN THE HISTORY

A ledger explains the balance.

GainCharges and corrections explain how the current amount owed arose. Several partial payments remain visible.
AcceptMore records than a single stored balance. A fee and payment can still be entered in one save.
04 / CATEGORY + NOTE

Enough structure for useful reports.

GainReliable totals by financial category, with one flexible note for labels and payees. No vendor administration.
AcceptFreeform names do not provide reliable per-payee reports. Those would need structured identities later.
05 / WHOLE RUPEES

Store the amounts the clinic uses.

GainInteger amounts make entry and calculation straightforward. An amount of 5000 means ₹5,000.
AcceptNo paise precision. Adjustments use signed amounts; other entry amounts are positive.
06 / TWO DATES

Separate the event from its recording.

GainentryDate is editable and drives financial reports and oldest-first coverage. createdDate records when the entry was made and maps to createdAt in storage.
AcceptBackdated entries can change earlier period totals and calculated coverage. The recording timestamp stays unchanged.
Room to grow

Itemize a charge when the clinic needs it.

An embedded breakdown can explain treatments, materials, and medicines within a charge, without changing receipt handling or adding debt twice. Formal invoices need a later decision about numbering and correction history. The current model remains the first step.
